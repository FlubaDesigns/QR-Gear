CLAUDE HANDOFF — STEP 3: STOREFRONT SIMPLIFICATION (BROWSE VS DECIDE) — THIS ZIP ONLY

This is the THIRD MOST IMPORTANT fix before launch.

Step 1 makes the member/public surface real.
Step 2 makes the member area trustworthy.
Step 3 makes the STOREFRONT feel inviting, easy to navigate, and much less friction-heavy.

Do NOT redesign the store architecture.
Do NOT change the store/channel/collection/product hierarchy.
Do NOT create a second storefront system.
Do NOT remove product detail pages.
Do NOT flatten everything into one giant page.

This is a UX SIMPLIFICATION pass using the current structure.

==================================================
PRIMARY GOAL
==================================================

Separate:
- BROWSE
from
- DECIDE

Right now the storefront still mixes:
- browsing
- configuring
- buying

too early, especially on collection/listing pages.

The target model is:

Store / Channel / Collection pages
= BROWSE

Product page
= DECIDE + BUY

This is the key correction.

==================================================
CURRENT PROBLEM (FROM THIS ZIP)
==================================================

Collection/listing pages still carry too much cognitive load.

Product cards are still doing too much or are too close to doing too much:
- too much information density
- too much interaction too early
- too much configuration pressure before the product page

The result:
- mobile browsing feels heavier than it should
- the store feels more “builder-shaped” than “shopping-shaped”
- users have to think too early instead of just browsing

This is one of the biggest public-facing friction points in the entire product.

==================================================
PART 1 — MAKE COLLECTION PAGES BROWSE-ONLY
==================================================

This is the main fix.

Pages like:
- /shop/internal/qrgear/usa250/armed-forces
- and equivalent collection/listing pages

must become clearly browse-first surfaces.

That means product cards on these pages should ONLY do the job of helping the user:
- notice an item
- understand what it is
- see the price
- open the product page

Required product card contents on collection/listing pages:
- one main image only
- product title
- price
- optional simple badge if important
- tap/click behavior to open product page

Do NOT keep heavy decision UI on collection cards.

==================================================
PART 2 — REMOVE CONFIGURATION FROM LISTING CARDS
==================================================

On collection/listing pages, REMOVE from product cards:
- color selectors
- size selectors
- option dropdowns
- add-to-cart controls
- embedded heavy gallery behavior
- multi-step selection interactions

Main rule:
If the user has to make a meaningful choice, it belongs on the PRODUCT PAGE, not the listing card.

This is especially important on mobile.
The listing page should feel fast and calm, not interactive in a stressful way.

==================================================
PART 3 — PRODUCT CARDS MUST BECOME ENTRY POINTS, NOT MINI PRODUCT PAGES
==================================================

Treat StoreProductCard (or equivalent listing card component) as an entry point.

The card should answer only:
- what is it?
- what does it cost?
- does it look appealing enough to tap?

It should NOT attempt to become:
- a mini configurator
- a mini cart form
- a mini gallery system
- a mini decision workflow

If there are multiple images, you may support:
- a subtle image count indicator
or
- a very light secondary cue

But do NOT embed a full heavy gallery experience in every listing card.

The full gallery belongs on the product page.

==================================================
PART 4 — PRODUCT PAGE BECOMES THE TRUE DECISION PAGE
==================================================

All meaningful interaction must happen on the product page.

The product page should be the only place where the user:
- explores the full image gallery
- selects color
- selects size
- reads deeper details
- adds to cart

This is where the richer interaction belongs.

Required behavior on the product page:
- full gallery/media set
- clearer product detail layout
- option selection UX
- add-to-cart flow

The storefront should guide users naturally:
browse on collection page
→ decide on product page

==================================================
PART 5 — KEEP THE HIERARCHY INTACT
==================================================

Do NOT change the hierarchy:
- Store
- Channel
- Collection
- Product

This pass is not about changing routing or architecture.
It is about making each layer feel like its proper role.

Role definitions:

STORE ROOT
- orientation and direction

CHANNEL PAGE
- themed destination and collection entry

COLLECTION PAGE
- product browsing

PRODUCT PAGE
- product decision and purchase

Do not blur these layers again.

==================================================
PART 6 — MOBILE-FIRST REQUIREMENTS
==================================================

This pass must prioritize mobile.

On mobile, collection/listing pages should:
- scroll fast
- feel light
- avoid dense cards
- avoid hidden or nested interactions
- use large card tap targets

The user should be able to:
- skim products quickly
- tap a product confidently
- land on the product page to make decisions

If listing pages still feel like work on mobile, the pass is not complete.

==================================================
PART 7 — VISUAL SIMPLIFICATION OF LISTING CARDS
==================================================

Reduce clutter in collection/listing cards.

Desired card feel:
- calm
- clear
- scannable
- image-forward

Avoid:
- too many controls
- too much small text
- too many stacked interaction zones
- too many competing visual elements

Use spacing and hierarchy to make the card easy to parse fast.

The goal is not “minimal for minimal’s sake.”
The goal is:
less friction, faster recognition, easier tapping.

==================================================
PART 8 — DO NOT BREAK PRODUCT OPTION LOGIC, JUST MOVE IT
==================================================

This pass is NOT Step 4 (product option UX redesign), but it must prepare for it correctly.

That means:
- remove option selection from listing cards
- keep option selection on product page
- do not destroy the underlying option system
- do not break add-to-cart logic

You are moving decision UI to the right place, not deleting product options.

==================================================
PART 9 — DO NOT BREAK MEDIA / GALLERY LOGIC, JUST RELOCATE THE EXPERIENCE
==================================================

If the product has rich media:
- keep it on the product page
- do not force it into collection cards

You may keep a single strong hero image on cards.
But the full gallery experience belongs on product detail.

This helps:
- performance
- clarity
- mobile usability
- perceived simplicity

==================================================
PART 10 — KEEP THE STORE INVITING
==================================================

This fix is not only about less interaction.
It is about making the store feel easier to enter.

After the pass, a user should feel:
- “I can browse this”
not
- “I have to work through this”

This means:
- cleaner collection pages
- clearer card hierarchy
- easier movement into product pages
- less tension before decision time

==================================================
PART 11 — WHAT NOT TO DO
==================================================

Do NOT:
- redesign the whole storefront
- change the store/channel/collection routing
- create a second product-card system unless necessary
- move heavy business logic into page files
- keep options/add-to-cart inside listing cards
- replace the product page with quick-buy logic everywhere

This is a simplification pass, not a rebuild.
